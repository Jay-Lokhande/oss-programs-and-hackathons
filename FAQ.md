# FAQ

Short answers — always read each program’s **official** rules before applying.

## Eligibility

**Do I need to be a CS student?**  
No for most programs. GSoC and ESoC care about contributions, not your major. Outreachy has its own eligibility rules. Summer of Bitcoin requires **university student** status.

**Do I need to be 18+?**  
GSoC, Outreachy, and most paid programs: yes. Some high-school hackathons (MLH, Hack Club) allow younger builders — check each event.

**Can I apply if I’m not in India / the US / the EU?**  
GSoC, Outreachy, ESoC, and SoB are global (with exceptions on official sites). OSPP is China-focused. C4GT and FOSSEE are India-focused.

**Can I do two programs at once?**  
Usually **no** for full-time summer programs (GSoC + Outreachy overlap). Hackathons and 24 Pull Requests can run alongside prep work. Read each contract.

## Programs vs hackathons

**Is GSSoC or Hacktoberfest enough for GSoC?**  
They help show activity, but selectors want **merged work on the org you apply to**. Treat GSSoC/SSoC as ramp-up, not a substitute for target-org PRs.

**Are hackathon wins equal to GSoC?**  
Different signal. Hackathons = prototype + teamwork. GSoC/SoB = sustained commits over months. Do both if you can.

**Is Outreachy only for coding?**  
No — UX, docs, data, community, and other tracks exist.

## Money & logistics

**Which programs pay the most (typical)?**  
Outreachy **$7,000** (fixed). GSoC/SoB/LFX use **location-based** tables — often **~$3k–$7k** equivalent. C4GT cites **~₹1L** per cycle.

**Summer of Bitcoin — paid in crypto?**  
Default is **BTC**; some regions allow local currency exceptions (official guide).

**Are these jobs?**  
Mentorships are **fixed-term stipends**, not permanent employment. Igalia Coding Experience and Red Hat internships are closer to jobs.

## Applications

**When should I start contributing?**  
**3–6 months** before applications for GSoC/Outreachy/SoB. See [resources/when-to-apply.md](resources/when-to-apply.md).

**I was rejected — now what?**  
Keep contributing to the same org, ask mentors for feedback, reapply next cycle. Many acceptances are second-try.

**Where are scams?**  
See [resources/scams-and-fake-internships.md](resources/scams-and-fake-internships.md). Fake GSoC portals, paid “guaranteed selection”, Telegram recruiters, and crypto “registration fees” are common. Real programs **never** charge you to apply.
